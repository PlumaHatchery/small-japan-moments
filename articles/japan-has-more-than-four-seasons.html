Seasons

Japan has more than four seasons

Spring, summer, autumn, and winter are only the broadest outline. Japanese seasonal culture often looks closer.

Quick answer

What are Japan’s 72 microseasons, or 72 seasons?

They are short traditional seasonal windows, each lasting about five days. People often search for them as “72 seasons,” “72 microseasons,” or “shichijūniko.” For readers outside Japan, the easiest way to understand them is not as a fact list, but as a calmer way to notice small changes in weather, flowers, food, and daily mood.

If you like the idea visually, Madobe turns the microseason rhythm into a quiet iPhone widget.

In Japan, seasonal feeling can appear in small changes: the first warm wind of spring, the sound of insects at night, a particular flower blooming, or a food that returns for only a short time.

One traditional way to notice these changes is the calendar of 72 microseasons. Each one lasts only about five days and points to a small shift in nature or daily life.

You do not need to memorize all of them to enjoy the idea. The important part is the habit of attention: looking for the small sign that today feels different from last week.

That habit is also why seasonal Japanese design can feel so gentle. It often leaves room for quiet changes instead of announcing everything loudly.
